Serving on the Palm Hill Board of Directors

Nov 1, 2026Listen
← Back

Written by Carolyn Story

A board member is the guardian of your community, assets, and lifestyle. Our primary duties are protecting assets, encouraging fairness, and planning for the future. Our legal obligations — called fiduciary duties — consist of the following:

1. Duty of Care: Acting with the same common-sense prudence you would use when managing your own affairs.

2. Duty of Loyalty: Always making decisions based on what is best for the community as a whole — never for personal gain or for a small group of friends or neighbors.

3. Duty of Obedience: Enforcing the association's governing documents. Time commitment can vary. Some factors include which committee you're directed to as the board liaison, projects that arise throughout the year, and how much time you personally are willing to give to your community.

I have been appointed liaison to the Golf Advisory Committee, of which I have co-chaired and been a member for numerous years. As liaison, I attend all meetings, acting as a communicator and governance bridge to the full board of directors. I do not partake in voting, and only join discussions when asked for thoughts, recommendations, or guidance. I am there in an advisory and supportive capacity for the committee. In turn, I present the committee's recommendations, proposals, or requests each month at the Board of Directors meeting.

I have served on the Communication Committee, Golf Advisory Committee, and Real Estate Committee, both as a member and as chair. I enjoyed all aspects of this work, including the orientations for new shareholders. I have served on your Board of Directors for six years, including a term on the executive committee as secretary. Last year, I ran again and was elected to a three-year term.

I find great satisfaction in serving my community. I've always felt that your first year serving should be like a sponge — absorbing the ways and thoughts of your fellow directors. This allows you to understand the work and see where you can best contribute to the team. When a vote is taken, majority rules; if the outcome isn't to your liking, go along with the vote as taken. You are part of a team working together, not an individual. Honestly, open communication is a powerful tool. Be available for your fellow shareholders. Be willing to listen and work together toward a reasonable solution.

As a cooperative, we only work as well as the members who step up to guide us. Serving gives you a direct voice in preserving Palm Hill’s policies. I encourage those who have the desire to step up to the plate to submit your letter of intent and join the Board of Directors — I'm sure there's a place for you on Palm Hill’s board. If you have any questions, I or any board member would be happy to talk about it with you.
