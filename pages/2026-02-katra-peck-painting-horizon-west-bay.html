Katra Peck’s Painting Chosen for Display in Horizon West Bay Complex in Downtown Largo

Feb 1, 2026Listen
← Back

Story and photos by Susan Kerr, LINK Editor

Palm Hill resident Katra Peck is a self-taught artist whose artwork attracted the attention of the City of Largo as a permanent fixture in the soon-to-be-opened Horizon West Bay complex in downtown Largo.

Katra’s artwork was spotted at the Largo Community Arts Center, where Largo officials were seeking local artists’ works to display in the new City Hall complex under construction.

“We specifically sought artists whose modern abstract styles aligned with the building's design. While some of the art is from out of state, we primarily focused on local and regional artists. We were thrilled to discover Katra Peck's work through the Largo Art Association,” said Kate Oyer, Communications and Engagement Director for the City of Largo.

“We selected and purchased an original piece from Katra, which will be displayed in the office space, most likely on the second floor. Although we will also have a rotating gallery in the lobby, Katra's work is one of approximately 40 pieces purchased for the interior office space to be permanently displayed. Two other Largo artists are Georgia Wildrick and Leslie Jeffery,” Ms. Oyer said. She said the downtown complex is expected to open the center in the Spring.

The 30 by 40-inch abstract painting purchased by the City of Largo was inspired by the rocks at Sand Key, said Katra. (A photo of the chosen painting was not available to the LINK, but will be on view this Spring at Horizon West Bay.)

Note: From the City’s Press release: Horizon West Bay will be the new home of City Hall and will be a mixed-use development designed to spark economic growth, strengthen community connections and bring new energy to the West Bay Drive corridor. It will include public gathering spaces, including night markets, live music, art pop-ups and seasonal celebrations.

Creative Art Space Attracts Palm Hill Residents on Tuesdays in South Rec Hall

Katra also has organized an art space for Palm Hill residents that meets on Tuesdays in the South Rec Hall. As many as 14 residents have come on Tuesdays from 1 to 4 p.m. to the South Rec Hall sessions, which offer a quiet and peaceful place to work, Katra said. She invites Palm Hillers to bring their supplies to enjoy the art space. The ample amount of tables and chairs in the South Rec Hall allows artists to work alone, spread out, or work in small groups. (The sessions are for artists, not crafters.)

In the past few years in St. Petersburg, Katra has been involved in the Warehouse art district monthly shows and in the Five Deuces Art Gallery juried monthly shows, as well as at the Beach Art Association shows in Indian Rocks Beach, where she won the “People’s Choice” award.

She has long been involved in art communities where she has lived, and is happy to be facilitating the creation of an “art space” in Palm Hill. “Come and see what we are doing,” said Katra, who is looking forward to more residents discovering the peace and camaraderie of a community of artists here in Palm Hill.
