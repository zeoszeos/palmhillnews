The President’s Corner

What follows is a report to shareholders about a few recent developments of interest on the regulatory and litigation fronts.

Regulatory:

First, I am forwarding by way of inclusion in this message my July 3rd email to the Board of Directors. It reports the results of an investigation by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). This is the state agency that regulates cooperatives and condominiums. The investigation was initiated by a complaint filed against the Association and implicating Mr. Newport, a current Board member and our past Board President. Mr. Gregg Hanke, another current Board member, authored and filed the complaint in mid-March.

Because of delays related to the Covid-19 emergency we did not learn of it until mid- April when we received a letter from DBPR. This letter did not disclose the author or actual text of the complaint. I reported this development to the Board at the April 23rd Board meeting.

In mid-May we were able to obtain a copy of the complaint from DBPR. It revealed that Mr. Hanke was its author. I sent a copy of the complaint to our Board members shortly after receiving it. Our legal counsel represented the Association in dealing with DBPR.

On June 30th our legal counsel received what is called a warning letter from DBPR advising us of the results of the investigation. In parentheses below is my report to the Board. It provides context and explains the investigation results. The DBPR is not seeking to impose any kind of fine or penalty on the Association.

“Gentlemen and Mrs. Story,

Attached please find a letter from the Florida DBPR to Palm Hill regarding a recent investigation involving the annual employee holiday lunch catered by Olive Garden.

The DBPR has taken the position in this letter and in conversations with our attorney that the employee holiday lunch expense is not technically a “common expense” as defined by Section 719.107(a), Florida Statutes, and in the future, the Board should not incur this expense unless the corporation’s governing documents are amended to specifically allow it.

As you will observe in reading the complaint filed by Mr. Hanke he did not address and was not in the least bit interested with the propriety of expenses related to an employee Christmas lunch being hosted by the corporation. Rather, he was interested in having Mr. Newport investigated for and accused of embezzling corporate funds for treating himself and a “few” board member friends to a dinner.

Mr. Hanke filed the complaint against the Association and named Mr. Robert Newport as the person against whom the complaint was filed and alleged as follows: “Document No. 1 is for diner (sic) at Olive Garden on December 21 for $382.16 that the Board President used Palm Hill Country Club credit card taking a few board members to diner (sic) without approval then hiding the expense under office supplies acct # 5011.” After identifying financial documents, he then declared “I believe this is misappropriation of corporate funds.”

As you know this accusation is demonstrably and patently false. The $382.16 payment to Olive Garden was for catering the employee Christmas luncheon held on 20 December at the South Clubhouse. The Park Manager paid Olive Garden for this service with the corporate credit card and coded the expense in the normal course of business.

I don’t know whether Mr. Hanke simply made up the preposterous story about a 21 December dinner at Olive Garden and sprinkled in a few financial documents to make it sound convincing to State authorities, or he just didn’t care or have enough common sense to verify a single fact of what he alleged. In his mind it may be that it just didn’t matter. It may be that in his calculations nothing, certainly not the truth, was going to get in the way of maligning Mr. Newport and getting him removed from the Board of Directors. It is no secret that Mr. Hanke was one of the principal leaders in the recall effort against Mr. Newport that began in August 2019 and which was discontinued in February 2020. His complaint to DBPR was filed a few short weeks after the recall effort was reportedly abandoned.

Again, please note that the DBPR did NOT find that the prior board president, Robert Newport, did anything improper whatsoever. Although the initial complaint included the outlandish allegations against Mr. Newport, the DBPR did not, understandably, find any merit in those accusations.

As you know, the employee holiday lunch has been held at Palm Hill for many years, under several boards and board presidents, and it has never been questioned until now. Actually, it was not even challenged or questioned in this instance. More appropriately, it’s having to be discontinued is collateral damage as it was only when the reason for the expenditure was explained to DBPR by legal counsel that DBPR focused any attention on it. Additional and more immediate damage of course is the approximate $3,000 that we have to date incurred in legal fees.

Again, the DBPR’s determination is limited solely to the issue of whether the use of Palm Hill funds for an employee holiday lunch is a statutorily-approved expense, and what we must do in the future, should we chose to do so, to continue to provide this courtesy and benefit to our hard-working and dedicated employees.

The DBPR has directed that a response to the Department be made by July 6, 2020. To that end I have noticed a closed meeting of the Executive Committee for Monday 6 July in order to consult with legal counsel and reach a timely decision about how to respond to the Warning letter.

Craig Mayer
President
HOPH, Inc.”

At the July 6th meeting the Executive Committee voted unanimously for legal counsel to send a response to DBPR representing that Palm Hill would not dispute the DBPR warning letter and to also represent to DBPR that the complaint’s allegations were false and believed to be filed by the complainant in bad faith.

Litigation:

On the same day our legal counsel received the letter from DBPR he also received a set of pleadings in the lawsuit the Association had settled last year with the Olingers. The pleadings seek to vacate the Pinellas County Court Order approving the settlement agreement

At the April 23rd Board meeting I mentioned that the Association had received a formal records request from Mr. Hanke’s lawyer on behalf of Mr. Hanke seeking documents about the Olinger case and that this would involve the Association incurring legal expenses. Mr. Hanke and the Olingers share the same lawyer. The pleadings we received refer to the document request but do not reveal the client in whose name (Mr. Hanke) the requests were actually made.

According to a statement our legal counsel received in November 2019, from the Olinger’s new lawyer, he undertook an attorney-client relationship with Mr. Hanke and Mr. Foti in October 2019. Clearly, because of this relationship and the activity by Mr. Hanke, he is effectively an opposing party with respect to the Association in the Olinger legal matter. It would appear that Mr. Hanke has been actively helping with the preparation of this motion.

The Executive Committee determined unanimously at its closed meeting on July 6th that the Association would oppose the motion and so instructed the Association legal counsel. This will involve additional legal expenses for the Association.

Craig Mayer
President
HOPH, Inc.
