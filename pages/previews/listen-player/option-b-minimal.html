A Profile of Palm Hill Resident Marge Taylor
Written by Carolyn M. Story, as told by Marge Taylor · Golf Advisory Committee

I was pleased with the opportunity to spend time with Marge Taylor, a 90-year-old golfer and Palm Hill resident since 1991, and to talk with my good friend of 35 years about her thoughts and experiences on the golf course.

Marge’s love affair with golf began in 1980, when she and a friend enrolled in a night class taught by a physical education teacher. It marked the beginning of a passion that enriched her life as it taught her patience and perseverance. As she puts it, “You’ve got to have it, or forget it!”

Since that time and over the years, Marge’s perspective on her favorite pastime has changed little, but her approach to the game has realigned. The ball doesn’t go quite as far, and she chooses more club, but that hasn’t stopped her from being on the links as often as she can these days. She loves the fresh air, friendships, and the game itself, though she admits it can be frustrating at times. Her advice: “Get out and play, forget about everything else, and just enjoy it.”

These days, because of her inconsistent balance, she’ll move the ball off a slanted slope for her own safety, and she leaves balls hit into the sand traps rather than risk an unsafe position and drops a new one elsewhere. You do what you have to do to continue to enjoy every day of life. Additionally, she added her wisdom by stating, “Don’t make too much of it; remember, the main goal is to have fun.”

“When you’re playing, be prepared and ready when it’s your turn to hit. You’re the only one thinking about your score, and at this stage of the game, it’s really not that important. What matters is the friendships, the weather, taking out a little frustration on the golf ball now and then, and mostly just having fun while staying strong and healthy. Golf keeps you socially active, keeps your mind sharp, and keeps your friendships intact. It mirrors life’s ups and downs.”

Marge and her husband Jack’s most memorable course was Waterway Hills in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina. This course presented many difficult and strategic shots due to the numerous holes surrounded by water. The couple also enjoyed the challenging rolling hills of the golf courses in Ashland, Kentucky, where they lived before retiring to Palm Hill.

Her first hole-in-one came on our Palm Hill Golf Course, and she followed that success 16 additional times. Last year alone, she scored two holes-in-one just two weeks apart, playing in a scramble with Bill Conners both times.

In the early years, Jack and Marge played golf daily with Carolyn and Richie Story, often logging 36 holes a day—or at least a minimum of 18. The next group they played with included Jim & Sandy Jones, Jackie & Arnie Klock, Carmen and Bob Smith, and Helen & Barry Spinney. Another group of golfing partners includes Arnold Toney, Kenny Smock, and Norm Bufford during the summer months, while Russ Bonfessuto is her golf Couples and Scramble partner during the winter months. She has made many friends along the fairway.

Her most unexpected day of golf happened in Palm Hill as she and 3 other ladies approached Hole #6, looked south, and couldn’t believe what they appeared to see: an enormous flock of birds, like something out of an Alfred Hitchcock movie. It turned out to be a tornado, hurling debris (not birds) through the air, without a drop of rain. They quickly headed for the safety of their homes. Carports in the south area of the community were severely damaged in the storm that day.

Marge has been fortunate to call Palm Hill home for 35 years. She credits her longevity to a healthier lifestyle, warmer weather (she’s originally from Buffalo, NY), staying active mainly through golf, and her many friends. “I want to thank all my friends who have played golf with me over the years.”

We thank you in return, Marge Taylor, for your perseverance and tenacity. You have earned the title of “Queen of the Greens.”
