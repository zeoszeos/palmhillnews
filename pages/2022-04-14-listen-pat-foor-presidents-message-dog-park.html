A Message from Board President Pat Foor

President’s Corner

Apr, 2022

The purpose of this message is to address the recent controversy regarding the establishment of a dog park or off-leash area in the park for dogs to run free. Between the north and south areas of the park exists what is called the 16th Avenue corridor. Residents can recognize it as the area where the large purple pipes are located. The Park owns this strip of land and in 2014 the City of Largo was granted a permanent easement through it by the Taylor family and the Association for possible construction of a wastewater transmission and collection system. The strip is divided by Royal Palm Circle road. To the East of the road is an open area where dog walkers have walked their dogs for many, many years and is designated in our Rules as a pet walking area. On the west side is the garden area which has been used for gardening activity for at least 30 years and probably much longer. The garden area is a gated and fenced area bordered on the north by Palm Hill residences, on the south by the Largo City waste water treatment plant, and on the west by city property and the Ridge/Clearwater Largo Road.

There are 31 garden plots in the garden area that run alongside the north boundary which is also the park’s white vinyl fence. Most of the plots are separated by wood planks. There are water spigots along the south side of the plots fed by the park’s irrigation water system. The site also houses a shed where Garden Club equipment and supplies are stored as well as a large water barrel which stores water for weekend use when the park’s water irrigation system is turned off. The water spigots, their water lines and the shed were installed and financed by Palm Hill in past years from operating funds.

Dog walkers began using the garden area a few years ago to walk dogs and to also allow them to run loose in the area as this is convenient inside the fence. Increased use of the garden area for these purposes was also probably being experienced because of the pandemic. This resulted in conflicts and potential conflicts between the dog walkers and the gardeners with respect to dog waste, presence of gardening pesticides, as well as loose dogs in or near gardeners.

In an effort to address these conflicts and incompatible uses, In Apr, 2021, the Board voted to allow use of the area by dog owners in the 16th Avenue corridor on the east side of Royal Palm Circle. This was done on a preliminary basis as an accommodation to residents without a shareholder vote and without fully investigating the legal requirements and impacts on the HOPH budget or insurability. Fencing supply, design, and installation issues related to not wanting to use fencing that might interfere with the City easement resulted in the fencing selected not arriving until several months later in the fall of 2021, and then only being partially erected. The fencing and park layout was unacceptable to dog owners because of its design and to nearby residents whose homes backed onto the corridor area where the proposed park fence was located.

The Park Manager put a dog park sign on the garden area gate in response to requests from dog owners who wanted to let their dogs run loose in the garden area on the west side of the road. This was not Board approved. Again, conflicts arose between residents using the area for their dogs and those using it for gardening. In early 2022 the “dog park” sign was removed at my direction as Board President. Thereafter, in mid-Feb, following an unauthorized private lock being placed on the gate for a day or two and then removed, a sign was placed on the gate advising that access was under the control of the Park. This sign was torn down. The Park then placed a combination lock on the gate.

After further research and investigation into what is involved in and required for the establishment of a dog park, the Board, at its Mar 9, 2022 meeting rescinded the Apr, 2021, motion to establish a dog park, and directed the removal of the erected fencing and its return for a store credit. The Board also, as authorized by our governing documents, adopted a rule restricting the garden area to gardening activity only with access, by way of a combination lock, regulated through the Park office.

In our more recent research members of the Board learned that the factors and considerations in establishing a dog park are much more involved than the Board originally realized or recognized. These included serious insurance coverage concerns, Largo City permitting requirements, and the likelihood that establishing a new recreational facility in conformance with our governing documents and state law would require at least at 50% vote of approval by unit owners. Moreover, our ability to even get any insurance or liability insurance coverage is affected if we have a dog park and its presence limits insurance carriers who are willing to provide us coverage. As the years have passed, fewer and fewer insurance companies are willing to get involved in insuring Parks such as ours. In relation to our Park this is even more of a concern as a result of the personal injury lawsuit filed against the Park by one of its residents three years ago. The suit was settled, however not before our then insurance company chose not to renew coverage and our insurance costs since have doubled, much of which is a result of that claim. Under these circumstances the idea of the Park taking on greater or more exposure to potential liabilities is not inviting from a business or fiduciary perspective.

For those who might be interested the city of Largo has a dog park on 5th Avenue which is located about 4 blocks from the 8th Avenue entrance to the Park.

A group of dog owners has now organized as the Pet Owners of Palm Hill to communicate with those interested in pets and related topics. Their very recent request(s) for information have been provided by office staff and they indicated that it would be shared with their attorney. This was followed by a later communication disavowing that they have an attorney, but still noting that some of its members do and that the new organization can’t control these members.

Allow me to reassure you, the shareholders, that the Board takes it responsibilities seriously and that we endeavor to abide our governing documents and to faithfully follow the law in order to protect Palm Hill and all its owners. As attorney involvement and the veiled threat of litigation has been invoked we will be relying on our legal counsel to guide us in determining next steps as we move forward to carry out our fiduciary responsibility.

Thank you,

Pat Foor
Board of Directors’ President
