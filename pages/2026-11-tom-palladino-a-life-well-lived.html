Story of the Month: A Life Well Lived

Nov 1, 2026Listen
← Back

I’m currently in search of Palm Hill Residents who fit into what I believe is a rare category. I’m learning that I have landed in a perfect community that proves longevity. Reaching 90 and remaining able to do things you love is reality here. I want to discover some secrets to this possibility.

I found out about Tom Palladino by chance or maybe as Tom would say, “It was luck.”

Early one morning, I posed a question to one of the golfers traveling by my yard. “Good morning. Do you happen to know anyone here in the park that still plays golf and is 90 or over?” He dropped a name. “Yeah, I know somebody. Uh, uh, uh, Tom Palladino. He still gets out here nearly every day and he’s a great guy.” So, I waited for an hour or so and called Tom. And he answered the phone. Tom understood right away my interest in creating a story. Now 90, Tom was ready to share his perspectives on what life has taught him. We arranged for time together so I could hear about the years I may never get to see. If I can at least hear different perspectives of how the story goes, I’ll be grateful.

Before I could ask any questions, Mr. Palladino began by informing me that, “Life goes by fast, enjoy it.” He followed his opening wisdom with tidbits from stories that prove his theory. All his memories seem to hold a common thread which he stated as, “I’m just lucky.” His easy-going, kind-natured attitude was immediately admirable. I wanted to learn more. “I just live a simple life and that’s all there is to it.” If there’s something I want, I buy it and I don’t worry about things.” I thought the interview might be over.

Tom then continued sharing the story of his life with a remarkable third-person kind of attitude.

He was born in the small town of Waterbury, Connecticut, and into a family with 10 siblings; 5 brothers and 5 sisters. His parents worked hard to feed all their children and they never complained. His mother stayed at home to keep everyone straight and his father worked two jobs and grew a garden to provide additional food. Tom is the only remaining member of his family and feels lucky and grateful to have each day.

In 1957, Tom enlisted in the US Army on December 7. Soon after receiving his required immunization shots, Waterbury, CT, had a devastating flood that destroyed much of everyone’s personal belongings. Because of the flooding, Tom lost the paperwork that accurately detailed his shot record. He was required to repeat the series of necessary shots before being accepted for required Army training. Tom finally reported for military training in January of 1958 and was now told that 3 out of every 4 men would be serving the country in the US Navy instead. This was a fortunate turn of events and Tom served proudly in The Navy as an Airedale. After only serving for two years, and because of his willingness to serve in a different branch, Tom received an Honorable Discharge from the military and was sent home. According to Tom, this was another “lucky” event.

Continuing to think and talk about his life, Tom went back to his childhood and shared that his first love was the game of Duck-Pin Bowling. Tom secured a job in town at the Bowling Alley. At the ages of 12 and 13, he earned additional money for his family. Tom manually set the pins for the bowlers in each lane, and he earned 8 cents per lane and per game. Soon after, Tom spent his Saturdays playing with the bowlers as they challenged other Alley’s teams, went to neighboring towns and travelled into nearby states. After setting up pins and spending so much time watching how the top players approached the game, Tom became an accomplished bowler. Today, Tom has one remaining trophy from decades of tournament and individual success. His other 31 trophies were donated to a friend who ran the Special Olympics Program in Connecticut. (No mention of luck was suggested.)

Tom’s career started with his own business painting and applying wallpaper. He eventually continued this career at the suggestion of his town barber who had a relative in charge of hiring employees for the city. Tom was hired for a job with The City of Waterbury, and he remained there for 24 years. He retired at 64, started travelling to Florida, and started playing golf every day.

While on a vacation in Florida, Tom met friends who lived in nearby New Haven, Connecticut. The couple had travelled back and forth for years to enjoy vacations and both climates. After just three years, Tom asked his friends to be on the lookout for a great place to live in Florida. They agreed to help him and sited a home in Palm Hill. The cost of homes at that time in Florida was so low that Tom figured the property must be a dump. His friends quickly informed him that the community surrounding the home even had a golf course. Tom bought his current home, on Frangipani, sight unseen, and moved into Palm Hill in 2007. Sounds like a lucky guy.

Tom continued to share his grateful, “lucky”, attitude with Palm Hill. He acted unselfishly and volunteered for Bonnie Roberts, (being over 90 years of age), who was working from 8am to 4pm, 7 days each week as the Golf Pro Shop Employee. Bonnie needed someone to help her in a time of sickness; Bonnie had become too ill to work. For four months, Tom continued working with Bonnie’s integrity, showing great care for the community, and without compensation.

Being grateful for all that has happened in your life and being willing to share parts of that life with others may be all it takes to live well at 90. Thank you, Tom, for defining the term “living well” and sharing stories from your lucky life.

Written by Lisa Bria as told by Tom Palladino
