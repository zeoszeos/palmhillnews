A Message from Board President Craig Mayer

Presidents Corner - May 13, 2020

The purpose of this message is two-fold. First, it is to address community concerns about the Board’s choice on May 6th of coal tar as the sealant for the street sealing project scheduled to be done this summer. Second, it is to address Board member Mr. Carmen Foti’s renewed use of shareholder emails harvested from the website member directory for unsolicited political messaging about the same subject.

Street Project Sealant: Road sealing is a widely used practice throughout North America to extend the life of paved roads between major repair and repaving projects. Sealing includes needed repairs as well as sealant application. Since 2002 Palm Hill has done at least three such road sealing projects with the most recent occurring in the summer of 2015. On May 6th the Board approved a street sealing project for this summer. While a repaving project may occur in the future it is important now for roads to be sealed and repaired so we extend their life and allow for reserve funds to grow to more easily finance the much more expensive repaving project. That project is most likely to be undertaken some four to six years from now. Additionally, repairing and extending the life of the streets may prove much more important right now as the financial impacts of the Covid-19 crisis are unclear and may well require us to be watching our funds quite carefully in the years immediately ahead.

After the May 6th Board meeting vote to go forward with the project, questions were raised about the projects use of a particular material – coal tar sealant – and its potential hazards to human health. This product has two advantages: a lower cost and a longer durability. However, because of community concerns about the potential environmental hazard of the coal tar sealant, on the evening of May 7th, I directed that no commitments yet should be made to Tar Seal, the vendor company chosen for the project. I then chose to speak with the Tar Seal representative on Friday May 8th asking him to review his pricing for the alternative PMM sealant product which is more expensive than coal tar sealant. He did so and advised me late Friday afternoon that he could discount his Bid price for using the PMM product by $1,229. Thus, the project using that product is now bid to cost $79,000 as compared to the $70,000 cost using the coal tar sealant.

An email was sent from me at 7:41 AM on Saturday morning May 9th advising Board members about the price change and canvassing them about reconsidering coal tar sealant as the product choice and instead going ahead with the more expensive PMM product. Mr. Foti was included on that email. By 10 AM on Saturday morning all five board members who had voted to accept the lower priced bid with coal tar sealant indicated to me that they would be agreeable to a product change and the higher price. It is therefore my intention to call a Special Board meeting to be held on Monday, May 18th so that the Board may review and reconsider its May 6th decision.

Mr. Foti was a recipient of my inquiry and was copied on all of the Board member emails responding to my inquiry. The first group email blast on this subject that was sent from Mr. Foti occurred at 10:48 AM and the blasts lasted until at least 11:55 AM. At this time our webmasters have identified at least four groups of email blasts. Like those sent by Mr. Foti this past January during the Board election process, these email blasts were comprised of 100 emails to a set.

Mr. Foti’s email messaging had nothing to do with Board members signaling a willingness to alter their positions as that had occurred by 10 AM on May 9th. Board members, me included, came to recognize that personal health concerns and fears registered by residents and certainly heightened by the Covid-19 emergency along with the PMM product being a relatively safer sealant merged and brought a weight to bear to overcome the price and durability advantages of the coal tar sealant.

For the record, please note that coal tar base sealant routinely has been used to seal Palm Hill roads and parking lots with three or four separate applications occurring since 2002. Coal tar base sealant was used in all of these applications with the most recent being the summer of 2015.

Additionally, on March 19th 2015, the last time that there was a vote by the Board for accepting a street sealing bid, Mr. Foti voted in favor of using coal tar base sealant and accepting the bid from Tar Seal which is the same company the Board selected last week. The Board vote in 2015 was unanimous. Also, please note that the literature cited in Mr. Foti’s email blasts dates from March 20, 2012. Additionally, for more information, under “Coal Tar” in Wikipedia may be found a very thorough discussion of coal tar and its many uses and features.

Unsolicited emails: First, a disclaimer - Mr. Foti’s emails sent this past Saturday were not authorized or sanctioned by the Board of Directors and were not sent out over official communication channels.

Unfortunately, the Palm Hill community is once again contending with the disruptive activities of Board member Carmen Foti. In late March it came in the form of his disregard for the Covid-19 south clubhouse closure order and his refusal to surrender building/room keys to the Park manager which I reported at the April 23rd Board meeting. An account of that episode appears in the minutes of that Board meeting. At the May 6th Board meeting it came in the form of Mr. Foti ignoring the rulings of the Chair with the Board voting to warn him that another decorum offense would result in the penalty of his removal from the meeting. His behavior at that meeting can be viewed on the recorded webinar video. This time it comes in the form of his renewed use of unit owner emails in violation of the terms of use of the Palm Hill website member directory. The emails are self -explanatory.

Sadly, at this stage, it is nearly impossible for shareholders to block receipt of these unwelcome and invasive communications unless email addresses are changed. Persons who receive them may forward them and senders may easily use new email addresses to circumvent blocking. I wish there were a simple remedy for ridding oneself of these unsolicited political communications but I don’t believe that one exists. The true and irreversible damage to shareholders’ privacy and respect occurred with the creation and release into the cyber universe last January of the copied and hijacked member directory emails.

Further and unfortunately, there appears no direct legal action or Board action reasonably available to the Corporation to stop Mr. Foti from abusing the use of member directory emails. However, if message content includes matters such as threats, libelous statements, false accusations, or impersonations of official corporate messaging appropriate legal action can be taken against the sender(s).

Shareholders are of course free to register their concerns directly with Mr. Foti. This message serves to register my continuing concerns as Chairman and President of the Board of Directors.

Thank you for supporting Palm Hill,

Craig L. Mayer
President
HOPH, Inc.
