Proposed Memorial Day Service

On several occasions over the past half century I have been the guest speaker at Memorial Day services - recounting my experiences in the Vietnam War – carrying dead and dying Marines to medevac helicopters – remembering the last words of a young Sergeant. I know intimately why there is a Memorial Day. Never, however, did I imagine that one day I might be asking fellow Americans to not attend a Memorial Day service, but today I am asking just that.

I urge you in the interest of the health and well-being of the community to not attend the memorial service scheduled for 7 PM this evening that is being sponsored and promoted by Ms. Hope Barina and others on social media and otherwise I imagine. It is an open invitation to all residents and first appeared last evening about 7:30 PM on the Website Message Board.

If the Fallen could speak, I believe they would tell you that the greatest respect you could demonstrate in their honor this Memorial Day is to not gather together in their name in any group or parades. Rather, they would ask you to observe this Memorial Day in the privacy of your home with quiet solemnity and reflection. They would want you to protect yourself and each other as they protected you.

This request is in furtherance of the Board’s efforts these past two months to protect the Palm Hill Community from the ravages of Covid-19. This private memorial event has not been pre-approved or sanctioned by the Board with regard to the use of a common area. With its surprise invitation coming on a Sunday, and the night before it was to be held – no opportunity existed, even if the Association favored and desired to approve such an event to properly organize and manage it – particularly with safety responsibilities related to Covid-19 and the ever changing sets of rules and guidance being issued by state and county officials.

The holding of such an event at this time on Park property, on the spur of the moment, with indeterminable numbers of residents in attendance, and without Park prescribed protocols in place is rightly considered a hazardous activity in violation of Rule XI. D of the Palm Hill Rules. CDC guidelines counsel no gatherings of more than 10 people for organizations that serve high-risk populations such as elderly adults. Eight (8) out of ten (10) deaths from Covid-19 have been adults aged 65 years or older. Experts are learning that as many as 35% of infected people are asymptomatic – showing no symptoms.

Craig Mayer
President
HOPH, Inc.

Sent to Palm Hill residents by email on Monday, May 25, 2020, at 2:40 PM ET.
