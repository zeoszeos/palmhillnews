Insurance and Liability Facts related to Dog Bites

Paragraph 19 of the Palm Hill Master Form Exclusive Right of Possession Agreement titled “Insurance” requires the Corporation to “obtain casualty insurance on the premises which shall insure against loss as a result of personal injury occurring in the Park.” Moreover, Article V, paragraph 5.9 of the Bylaws specifies that it is the duty of the Board of Directors “To carry insurance for the protection of the shareholders and the Association.”

The foregoing duties exist irrespective of Florida Statute 767.04 which like many other states in the country makes dog owners strictly liable to victims for injuries caused by their dogs. That is all this statute does. It does not address dog owner insurance and it places no minimum insurance requirement on dog owners as, for example, is experienced with auto insurance. Neither does this statute limit responsibility for personal injuries caused by a dog exclusively to the dog’s owner.

A victim of a dog bite or dog attack, to include an injury to only the victim’s dog, could readily sue both the dog owner and an owner of a dog park where an injury occurred. It could be alleged that the dog park was operated negligently by not enforcing its rules, allowing dangerous dogs to use the Park, or failing to keep loose dogs inside a fence. Victims of attacks are likely to also seek recovery for injuries from a dog park owner if the owner of a biting dog does not have renter’s, homeowner, or special insurance covering dog bites or has too little coverage in terms of dollar value. Also, insurance coverage might not extend to injuries occurring outside the home or if the dog is considered dangerous because of its breed or prior behavior.

Dog owners should have insurance as any dog can bite. As noted above, Palm Hill must have personal injury insurance. Additionally, it is quite likely that a large number of the Park’s dog owners have no dog bite insurance or have insurance limits that are inadequate when it comes to typical claims. Assuming liability is established, claim settlements might well be shared between a dog owner and the Association.

Some other relevant facts are helpful to know in understanding and appreciating concerns about dog bite liability: In the United States as of 2022, according to the Insurance Information institute, there are nearly 85 million dogs living in U.S. Households, and about 4.5 million people are bitten by dogs every year. Dog bite or dog-related injury claims cost homeowner insurers about $882 million in 2021 according to the Institute. The average cost per claim nationwide in 2021 was $49,025. After California, which had 2,026, Florida had the second highest number of claims at 1,478 with an average cost per claim in Florida of $54,820.

With regard to Palm Hill’s personal injury insurance coverage, Cincinnati Specialty Underwriters (CSU) was selected after providing an extremely favorable quote, however, it would not cover a dog park or dog bites/attacks regardless of where they might occur in the Park with any of the standard coverage. Generally, as explained to Board members, insurers because of the risks involved do not like to cover dog parks or liability arising from dog bites or attacks. Many insurers refuse to even bid on any insurance for homeowner parks that have them. If they do bid, they will exclude the dog parks and dog bite injuries, charge very high premiums, or possibly provide severely limited coverage in a separate-type policy.

In the case of our insurer, CSU, because of the Board’s inquiries, it quoted or offered severely limited coverage for dog bite/attack injuries or what is known as sub-limit insurance at $10,000 per occurrence with an aggregate of $20,000. This means that the most that would be paid for any single bite or attack claim would be $10,000 and for the entire insurance year no more than $20,000 would be paid in total for any and all claims related to a dog bite. The cost for obtaining the sub-limit policy was quoted at $400.

In my capacity as President, and after a very recent inquiry to CSU, they declined increasing the sub-limit coverage limits, I have however directed the Management Company to obtain this limited coverage and put it into effect. It would cover potential liability up to the coverage limits in the event the Park is named in a lawsuit as being responsible or partly responsible for an injury. Notably, the insurance available through CSU would not cover the amount of a single average dog bite claim in Florida. This means that the park will be self-insuring from its own operating funds for any claims beyond the sub-limit insurance limits for any potential liability arising from a dog bite or attack in the Park.

Thank you,

Pat Foor
Board of Directors’ President
