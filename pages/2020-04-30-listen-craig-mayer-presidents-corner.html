A Message from Board President Craig Mayer

The Presidents Corner

Since my last remarks over a month ago we have witnessed the scourge of Covid-19 as it has continued to spread across the globe. With the death of two residents attributed to Covid-19 our community was not spared its worst effects and we all have been adjusting, regardless of where we may be living, to the sometimes harsh and certainly unfamiliar changes in our day to day lives. That said, however, we are now beginning to see authorities relax some of the lockdown restrictions as they prepare for a better but still uncertain future. It is now necessary for our community to adjust again.

What follows in italics are policy considerations to help frame and guide us in adopting the specific protocols and policies that we will have to implement and abide as we “reopen”. It has been earlier shared with all Board members and should help us all in discussions on May 6th at the workshop part of the Board meeting. At that meeting the Board will also discuss the recent security problems the community has experienced which are not unrelated to the Covid-19 crisis.

On Monday, 27 Apr we reopened the playing surface of the golf course. That included a carefully considered list of social distancing guidelines and rules. On Tuesday, 28 Apr Pinellas County announced the lifting of closure orders on beaches and swimming pools. On 29 Apr Governor DeSantis announced the partial relaxation of the State’s emergency Safer-at-Home Order to be effective 4 May. The Park Manager and the Board are reviewing the government orders that accompanied these actions along with recommendations from our management company and insurance company to determine what course of action is best for Palm Hill and within our capabilities. Decisions will be forthcoming and we expect to have something to report on Friday. Thank you for your patience as we sort through the many issues related to reopening the pools. The guidelines follow:

Palm Hill Covid-19 Reopening Protocols and Policies

Policy Guidelines: In crafting protocols we should first recognize the fears that they must address. In short these are 1) the fear of contracting Covid-19 and our own mortality, 2) the fear of losing our money, and 3) the fear of losing our community. To borrow from health community terminology, the “antibody” for these fears is a common purpose. For Palm Hill that purpose can be expressed as follows:

To continue to have Palm Hill be and be regarded as a highly desirable location and community for retirement styled living.

To that end I would offer up three guiding principles for the development and application of the protocols to restructure the conduct of Palm Hill’s social and business engagements for whatever period of time it is required. Prudently, we must: 1) exercise necessary public health precautions with the recognition that ours is a vulnerable retirement aged population, 2) not be so restrictive with precautions as to preclude the enjoyment of reasonably available activities and amenities, and 3) remain flexible in tightening or loosening the protocols or precautions as circumstances dictate. A common purpose as well is, of course, to have Palm Hill go through this ordeal without an unwanted outbreak of the Covid-19 virus and/or any additional loss of life.

It should also be acknowledged that Covid-19 protocols to some degree will need to be in effect for the indefinite future. Most likely, according to public health authorities, that could be for as long as two years and until safe and effective vaccines or treatment therapies are in place.

Pursuit of our goal and application of our reopening principles will of course be subject to the various requirements dictated by government authorities. At a minimum, we must comply with the applicable government requirements, but we are nonetheless free to adopt and apply more stringent protections as may be appropriate specifically because our vulnerable population is at risk for the most serious complications of Covid-19. Also, unless we undertake unbudgeted expenditures for adding more staff or contract personnel, we will have to rely primarily upon self-policing to achieve compliance with the protective measures we newly implement or continue to implement.

Finally, in our thinking about the protocols, it is useful to place them into categories and subcategories. The three main categories would be

1) Park Amenities
2) Park Business Matters/Operations, and
3) General Protective Measures.

The subcategories for Amenities would be 1) buildings and facilities, and 2) activities.

The subcategories for Business matters and operations would be the same two as for amenities but with the added subcategory of: 3) employees, contractors, or vendors.

Thank you for taking the time to read about the guidelines and thank you in advance for constructive recommendations or comments.

Thank you for supporting Palm Hill.

Craig Mayer,
President, HOPH, Inc
