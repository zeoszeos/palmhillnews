A Message from Board President Craig Mayer

February 15, 2020

President's Corner

The purpose of this communication is to address three subjects of community interest. First, the recommendations by the Special Investigative Committee that examined the events surrounding the election and that were submitted to the Board on February 5, 2020. Second, is the development of the Board of Director’s Policy and Governance Manual discussed briefly at the February 5th meeting, and third is the subject of “Workshop” meetings.

The Investigative Committee Report. Among the Committee’s findings and recommendations are several matters that can be addressed without delay and that do not require Board actions. First, is the unauthorized print order for ballot envelopes that occurred the week before the election. Mr. Foti accepted responsibility for placing this order. Simply put, it has been and remains the rule that print orders to the Association’s printer for any materials purporting to be those of the Association can only be placed or made by and through the Park office. No Officer, Director, Committee person, or Unit Owner may unilaterally or independently place such an order.

The Committee’s second recommendation was to design a Code of Conduct for Board members such that they might better appreciate the expectations that the community places in them. Already in existence are a Code of Conduct and a Disclosure of Interest policy. Both were adopted unanimously by Board resolution on March 18, 2015 and each appears as an appendix in the draft Policy and Governance Manual. Copies of each document were placed in Board members’ office mailboxes before the February 5th Board meeting. Additionally, on February 8th in an email, I directed the attention of all Board members to these documents and asked that they review them.

Recommendations numbered 3, 4, 7, and 8 address reforms to the election process to include designing ballots that cannot be easily replicated, developing a policy for providing assistance to disabled residents who may need such for vote casting, keeping the voting list current, and reviewing our management company’s role/responsibilities in the election process. Most of these recommendations will likely require Board actions and will be properly noticed on a meeting agenda and should be able to be addressed in the course of the next three scheduled Board meetings.

Recommendations 5 and 6 concern administrative privileges to access the website data systems and educating the community about website use. These are matters for the Communications Committee to address with recommendations as appropriate.

Again, my sincere thanks to the Investigative Committee for their time and efforts on behalf of Palm Hill. The report is considered an official record of the Association and may be viewed at the Park Office.

Board of Director’s Policy and Governance Manual. Copies of the Manual, currently in draft form, may be found in both the North and South Clubhouse libraries. Please don’t remove them from the libraries. Rather, please make any comments or recommendations on a separate sheet of paper(s) and submit that to the Park office. The intention is to have the first four (4) parts of the Manual adopted as soon as practicable. The first four parts of the Manual are titled: Part 1: Palm Hill Governance Documents and Laws Governing Cooperatives; Part 2: Board of Director Meetings; Part 3: Committees; and Part 4: Communications.

All current Board members and several past Presidents are reviewing or have reviewed the draft manual. At the same time, shareholders will have ample time to address any aspect of the proposed Manual during informal discussion periods at upcoming Board meetings. To this end, the Manual will be placed on the agenda for the February 26th Board meeting “for discussion only.” After any additions or changes are made as a result of the various reviews the Manual will then appear on the March 11th Board meeting agenda for any further discussion and action as appropriate.

Workshop Meetings: There is no requirement or mandate in our governing documents for the Board of Director’s to hold “workshop” meetings. Rather, the holding of meetings titled as “workshop” are discretionary. While they may be held by the Board, by Palm Hill Rule (Rule XXII. D.) the business of the corporation discussed at such a meeting cannot be acted upon or “carried out” until the next or another “Meeting of the Board of Directors.” This restriction can handcuff the Board when items of business need to be acted upon but find themselves in the queue for a meeting designated as a workshop. It appears that, over time, it simply became our custom for “workshop” meetings to precede every “Board” meeting irrespective of business needs.

The interests served by workshop meetings are in my view twofold. First, to allow more time and consideration by the Board and Shareholders with topics of significant interest or consequence, and second to allow for the Board to conduct a meeting at a location such as the accountant’s office or at the site of storm damage or construction where strict adherence to formal meeting procedures are not easily implemented or necessary.

Having longer lead times for publishing the notice of Meetings and agendas, posting some agenda items as “for discussion only,” having more informal discussion at regular Board meetings, and reserving workshop meetings themselves for particular events or subjects should serve our interests better than continuing to practice as routine or in lock-step the workshop then Board Meeting pattern.

Thank you for supporting Palm Hill,

Craig Mayer
President
HOPH, Inc.
